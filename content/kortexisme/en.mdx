## Preamble

We live in an age where knowledge is ever more accessible and technology is deeply changing the way we learn, think, create and interact with the world.

Artificial intelligence, digital tools and scientific advances open possibilities that were once hard to imagine. Yet this progress raises a fundamental question: how can we push the limits of our abilities without losing sight of the values that give our lives meaning?

This is the question **Kortexism** sets out to answer: a philosophy grounded in continuous learning, self-improvement, the responsible use of technology and an acknowledgement of the limits of the human condition.

Kortexism does not seek to make human beings all-powerful. It encourages everyone to develop the abilities they have been given, to acquire new knowledge and to use the tools of their time with intelligence, humility and responsibility.

## The origin and meaning of Kortex

The name *Kortex* is inspired by the cerebral cortex, the part of the brain involved in many cognitive functions, including reasoning, memory, language and perception.

Here, the cortex stands for human intellectual potential: our ability to learn, to understand, to imagine and to solve problems.

Technology, for its part, is a set of tools that widens our possibilities. Through it, we can reach more knowledge, explore complex fields and accomplish tasks that would sometimes be beyond our individual means.

> Kortex aims to be a bridge between human intelligence and the possibilities technology offers.

Kortexism holds that the current limits of our knowledge need not be the final frontiers of our potential.

## Our vision of progress

Progress is not measured only by the power of machines, the sophistication of software or the performance of artificial intelligence.

It is also measured by our ability to understand the world better, to pass on our knowledge, to sharpen our judgement and to act responsibly.

For Kortexism, technology must remain a means in the service of human beings, never an end in itself.

We encourage innovation, research, experimentation and intellectual curiosity. However, we recognise that a technical possibility is not automatically a moral justification.

> Everything that can be achieved technically deserves to be weighed with discernment before it is carried out.

## Our Christian inspiration

Kortexism, as we wish to conceive it, belongs to a worldview consistent with our Christian faith.

This inspiration rests on several convictions: humility before the vastness of knowledge, responsibility in the use of our abilities, respect for human dignity and the pursuit of wisdom.

We consider the acquisition of knowledge a precious endeavour, but one that must go hand in hand with a moral conscience.

Technology replaces neither faith, nor conscience, nor personal responsibility. It does not make human beings all-powerful, nor does it remove the limits inherent to their condition.

Our ambition, then, is not to play God, to claim to know everything or to seek absolute dominion over nature. It is to learn, to understand, to create and to contribute to the common good with the means at our disposal.

This Christian inspiration guides our thinking without making Kortexism a religion, a church or a substitute for faith.

## Preserve and surpass

Kortexism seeks a balance between two complementary attitudes.

On the one hand, it recognises the importance of preserving the foundations that give stability to human life: moral values, the passing on of knowledge, responsibility, human bonds and the principles we hold essential.

On the other hand, it refuses to treat habits, present difficulties or established standards as necessarily final limits.

To preserve is not to refuse all change. To surpass is not to reject all heritage.

It is about examining what we have received, keeping what remains precious and improving what can be improved.

> We want to evolve without losing our bearings, and to keep our bearings without giving up on evolving.

## The place of artificial intelligence

Artificial intelligence holds an important place in the Kortex vision, because it can ease access to knowledge, assist reasoning, speed up certain tasks and support creation.

However, we recognise its limits: errors, bias, inaccurate information, over-reliance and the risks of irresponsible use.

We therefore encourage an active and critical use of AI. The point is not to hand our thinking over to a machine, but to learn to work with it while developing our own judgement.

The goal is to make people more capable, more autonomous in their learning and better equipped to meet the challenges of their time.

## Who is a Kortexian?

A Kortexian is someone who embraces the principles of Kortexism and seeks to apply them in the way they learn, create and use technology.

You do not need to be an engineer, a scientist or an AI specialist to recognise yourself in this philosophy.

A Kortexian is first and foremost a learner: they cultivate curiosity, accept having their knowledge put to the test, build their skills and own their responsibilities.

They do not try to appear superior to others. They seek to grow, to share what they learn and to put their abilities to work for worthwhile goals.

> Being a Kortexian does not mean knowing everything. It means staying willing to learn.

## Our stance on transhumanism

Kortexism shares with some transhumanist approaches an interest in using science and technology to overcome certain human limits.

It is particularly interested in enhancing intellectual abilities through education, digital tools and artificial intelligence.

Yet this openness to progress does not mean that every human transformation is desirable, or that enhancement should be pursued at any cost.

Questions about biological transformation, brain–computer interfaces or profound modifications of the human being call for careful scientific, ethical and human reflection.

Kortexism therefore favours a measured approach: exploring what progress makes possible while weighing its consequences and recognising the limits of our control.

## Our commitment

We want to build a culture in which learning is a strength, curiosity a virtue, technology a tool and responsibility a requirement.

We want to encourage people to develop their skills, share their knowledge and use innovation to widen their possibilities.

We refuse to confuse mastery of a tool with wisdom, accumulated knowledge with omniscience, or technical power with moral superiority.

We believe it is possible to pursue progress without scorning heritage, to innovate without abandoning discernment, and to learn without claiming to master everything.

## Conclusion: surpass without losing ourselves

Kortexism rests on a simple conviction: the limits of our knowledge can be pushed back, but progress must remain accompanied by humility, responsibility and discernment.

We want to learn more, understand more deeply and use the possibilities of our time to create, to pass on and to contribute to the common good.

We do not claim to be all-powerful. We choose to keep learning.
